## Description and source

NTU RGB+D 120 holds 114 480 skeleton sequences captured by three Microsoft
Kinect v2 cameras at the same time (25 joints, camera coordinates in metres,
30 frames per second): 120 actions performed by 106 subjects in 32 setups
(camera height and distance), with 2 replications. RGB+D means colour (red,
green, blue) and depth.

- Reference: J. Liu, A. Shahroudy, M. Perez, G. Wang, L.-Y. Duan and
  A. C. Kot, "NTU RGB+D 120: A Large-Scale Benchmark for 3D Human Activity
  Understanding", *IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) TPAMI (Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine
  Intelligence)* 42(10), 2020,
  doi:[10.1109/TPAMI.2019.2916873](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2019.2916873).
- Source: <https://rose1.ntu.edu.sg/dataset/actionRecognition/>. Licence:
  ROSE Lab agreement (non-commercial research); manual download only.
- Local copy: `<data root>/SKELETON/NTU_RGBD_120/npy_skeletons_by_class/A<action>/S<setup>C<camera>P<subject>R<rep>A<action>.npy`.

## Getting the data

Manual access: account, form and Release Agreement on the
[ROSE Lab page](https://rose1.ntu.edu.sg/dataset/actionRecognition/). Download
`nturgbd_skeletons_s001_to_s017.zip` and `nturgbd_skeletons_s018_to_s032.zip`, then

```python
fetch_ntu120(archive=["~/Downloads/nturgbd_skeletons_s001_to_s017.zip",
                      "~/Downloads/nturgbd_skeletons_s018_to_s032.zip"])
```

The official `.skeleton` text files are read directly (`read_skeleton`, same
arrays as the local `.npy` copy, checked on 41 real files). Without `archive` the
fetch opens the page and raises `ManualDownloadRequired`. Copies re-published by
third parties go against the ROSE Lab terms and are not used.

## Raw data

Each `.npy` file is a dictionary with `skel_body0` … `skel_body3` (T, 25, 3),
`nbodys` (bodies per frame), `nframes`, … The setup, camera, subject,
replication and action are parsed from the file name. Only body 0 is used;
frames where it is lost (all coordinates zero) are dropped. The figure shows
the 15 part features of four sequences (feature × frame).

## Classes

120 actions, 683 to 960 kept sequences each. Coarse task (`task="coarse"`,
as in the paper): 82 daily actions (75 544 sequences), 12 medical conditions
(A041–A049, A103–A105: 11 292) and 26 mutual actions (A050–A060, A106–A120:
23 807). For mutual actions only body 0 of the two persons is used.

## Covariance construction

One symmetric positive definite (SPD) covariance per sequence, over its frames
with a body (`estimator="scm"`, the sample covariance matrix, by default).

- `features="parts"` (default, p = 15): per frame, the root-centred
  coordinates (joint 0, the spine base, is subtracted) are averaged over five
  body parts of four joints each: trunk (1, 20, 2, 3), left arm (5, 6, 7, 21),
  right arm (9, 10, 11, 23), left leg (12, 13, 14, 15), right leg (16, 17, 18,
  19) (0-based joints; the root, shoulders, hips and thumbs are left out). The
  atlas calls this covariance "C15". n ≥ 2p = 30 keeps 97 % of the sequences.
- `features="joints"` (p = 75): the raw 25 × 3 coordinates, as the former
  `utils/ntu120_covariance_precompute.py`. n ≥ 150 keeps only about 2.5 % of
  the sequences (most are shorter than 5 s): use it for long-sequence studies
  only.

Excluded and listed in `dataset.excluded` (default features): the **356 files
without any body** (`nbodys = 0`) and 3 481 sequences with fewer than 30 frames
with a body. The official list of the 535 defective samples of the paper is not
in the copy, so it is not applied. With `normalize=True` (default), covariances
are multiplied by the scalar fitted on the training part (geometric mean of the
eigenvalues = 1).

## Splits

The two official protocols of the paper are provided:

- `"xsub"` (cross-subject, X-Sub; also `"official"` and `"recommended"`): the
  53 training subjects listed by Liu et al. (`ntu120.TRAIN_SUBJECTS`) for
  training, the 53 others for test. On the whole copy: 63 360 / 51 120
  sequences (the paper gives 63 026 / 50 919 after removing its 535 defective
  samples); after the exclusions above: 61 911 / 48 732.
- `"xsetup"` (cross-setup, X-Setup): even setups for training, odd setups for
  test (54 720 / 59 760 on the copy).

The paper has no validation set. The validation part is 20 % of the training
**subjects** (X-Sub) or of the training **setups** (X-Setup), drawn with seed
0, never random sequences: one take is filmed by 3 cameras. Sizes with the
default features: X-Sub 43 926 / 17 985 / 48 732, X-Setup 46 341 / 6 673 /
57 629. Groups (`dataset.groups`) are the subjects; the figure shows the
number of sequences per subject.

## Leakage risks and measures

| Risk | Measure | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| same subject in train and test | X-Sub: subject lists of the paper; validation drawn by subject | none (tested) |
| same take seen by 3 cameras | validation never drawn among sequences | none |
| same setup in train and test (X-Setup) | setups disjoint, validation by setup | subjects shared across setups, by design of the protocol |
| defective samples | 356 files without a body excluded; the 535 official ones not available | some noisy sequences remain |

## Supported tasks

- `task="fine"`: 120 actions; `task="coarse"`: daily / medical / mutual.
- `drop_classes` (e.g. the mutual actions, whose second person is ignored),
  `merge_classes`, `classes`, `max_classes`, `max_samples_per_class`;
  `balance="undersample" | "oversample" | "weights"` (training part only).

## Usage

```python
from spdnet_datasets import create_dataloaders
from spdnet_datasets.skeleton.ntu120 import NTURGBD120Dataset

ds = NTURGBD120Dataset(subset=None, split="xsub", verify=None)   # verify=None skips the 117 567-file check
train, val, test = create_dataloaders(ds, split="xsub", batch_size=64)
coarse = NTURGBD120Dataset(subset="train", task="coarse")
```

## Migration notes

- The former loader read 75×75 covariances from `cov/` (3 086 matrices, 70

- `scaling_factor` is replaced by `normalize=True`.

## Covariance series

{class}`~spdnet_datasets.series.CovarianceSeries` cuts each skeleton sequence into a series of
covariance tokens (see {doc}`/guide/series` and {doc}`series_overview`). The modes and
their defaults come from the registry; the first one is the default.

```{series-table} ntu120
```

The skeletons are read again from the copy (`NTURGBD120Dataset.structured_raw`,
camera coordinates of body 0). Built by name, the loader keeps the sequences of the former
`real/series_ntu.py`: actions A001-A010, at most 300 per class (drawn by the loader, so not
the exact same draw), cross-subject split; the `axis` mode keeps the 11 long actions
(`NTU_LONG_ACTIONS`, median 123 frames), as `ntu_axis_series`. The coordinates of `part`,
`jointcov` and `axis` are standardised with the training sequences, per joint and coordinate
(`ntu_jointcov_series` used one isotropic scale per joint). `part_temporal` cuts 5 body parts of
5 joints (`p = 15`, the root shared by the trunk and the legs) in 4 windows; a token with
fewer than 30 frames is masked. `window_p75` and `window_p45` are the R23 series (Ledoit-Wolf,
`allow_small_n=True`).

Every default can be overridden by name:

```python
from spdnet_datasets.series import CovarianceSeries, collate_series

series = CovarianceSeries("ntu120", "frame")
series = CovarianceSeries("ntu120", "jointcov", n_windows=4, width=0.4)  # 4 windows of 40 % of the sequence
(tokens, mask), label = series[0]
```
